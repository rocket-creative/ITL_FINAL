## Body Copy (paste into Flodesk)

The FDA Modernization Act 2.0 and the agency's April 2025 roadmap have shifted the regulatory landscape for preclinical drug development. Nonclinical tests—organoids, organs-on-chips, computational models—are now explicitly authorized alongside animal studies. The announcement prompted urgent questions: Are mouse models becoming obsolete? How should researchers and model providers adapt?

In this blog, we explore what the new regulatory flexibility actually means, where New Approach Methodologies (NAMs) excel, and why mouse models remain scientifically indispensable for complex systemic pharmacology, immune-mediated responses, and chronic disease modeling.

**What's inside:**

- **Understanding the FDA's new direction** — What changed in the law, what the 2025 roadmap says, and what "regulatory flexibility" means in practice.
- **The promise of NAMs** — Organoids, organs-on-chips, and computational models: where they add value and where they fall short.
- **Critical limitations NAMs cannot yet overcome** — Absence of functional vasculature, missing immune systems, and insufficient systemic integration (with CAR T-cell toxicities as a case in point).
- **Why mouse models remain essential** — Humanized liver and immune system models, the FIAU lesson, and what the FDA still expects in IND submissions.
- **The path forward: integration, not replacement** — How regulators and researchers are advocating combined strategies that leverage both human-based technologies and genetically engineered animal models.

Ready to see how the regulatory evolution affects your preclinical strategy and why mouse models continue to underpin translational research?
